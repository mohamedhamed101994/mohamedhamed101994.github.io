Retail Sales Analytics & BI

End-to-end retail BI — from raw multi-year sales files to an interactive Power BI report.

Business problem

Turn three years of raw retail transactions into a reporting solution that explains sales performance across products, customers, stores and returns.

Data

A public retail dataset covering 1997–1999: about 145K orders, 10.3K customers, 1,559 products and 24 stores across the US, Mexico and Canada.

Key findings

2×Net revenue growth, 1997 → 1998 ($0.57M → $1.20M)
~2.5–3×Revenue per sq ft, large vs. small store formats
0.63%Return rate (of items sold)
  • Net revenue fell to $1.01M in 1999; January–April each ran 11–18% below the same month in 1998.
  • Clear Q4 seasonality: November and December are the strongest months.
  • Supermarkets and Deluxe Supermarkets generate about $7.4 per sq ft, vs. $2.6–2.9 for Small and Mid-Size Groceries.
  • The US contributes about 62% of net revenue.
  • Basket size is flat across weekdays, at 8.8–9.2 items.

Approach

  • Python (Pandas): merged and prepared the multi-year sales files, then loaded customers, products, stores and regions into SQL Server.
  • SQL Server: built fact and dimension tables and the business logic — order identification, sales amounts, product profitability and turnover, customer age segmentation, customer lifetime logic and validation checks.
  • Python analysis: explored revenue, orders, items sold, basket size and trends before modeling.
  • Power BI: Business Process, Executive Overview, Sales Trend, Sales, Products, Customers, Stores and Returns pages. A DAX Calculation Group lets one visual switch between Current, Previous Year, Growth, Growth %, YTD, MTD, QTD and Rolling 12 Months.
